# Room assignments — which surprise each room showcases

Every room names one entry from SURPRISES.md and shows ONE specific consequence of it.
Two rooms may cite the same entry only if they show different consequences, and the
consequence each room is responsible for is fixed here so they cannot collide.

## Row 0 — the high dry terraces. Timber scaffold, no standing brine, long falls.
- (0,0) S2 — that a hop across crust buys back its own airtime, so a heavy body crosses
  by hopping where it cannot cross by standing.
- (1,0) S11 — that where you let go of a swing decides everything, and what you weigh
  decides almost nothing.
- (2,0) S10 — that crust broken by your own weight leaves corners behind that were not
  there before, and those corners are the only way back up.
- (3,0) S8 — that a hook and a floor both wear out, and heavy makes the hook the thing
  that fails first.
- (4,0) S4 — that crust fails only under the feet, so a body that stops twice leaves two
  separate holes with good crust between them.

## Row 1 — upper pans. Shallow brine, iron lips, the first place weight is a choice.
- (0,1) S1 — that standing is what breaks crust and walking is not.
- (1,1) S5 — that a floating body sits at exactly the height a flush lip blocks, so you
  have to push up out of the water before you can get onto it.
- (2,1) S9 — the cascade: a hook that shears drops you onto a floor your own hanging
  had already fatigued.
- (3,1) S11 — that pulling your grip in whips you faster, and the launch angle is
  entirely a matter of when you let go.
- (4,1) S3 — that how deep you sit in brine is the only load counter in the game.

## Row 2 — the middle. (2,2) is the first room and is already authored.
- (0,2) S6 — that the brine's surface is a ceiling: swimming cannot lift you out of it.
- (1,2) S1 — the other half: that crossing crust without stopping is safe at any weight.
- (3,2) S10 — that a sheared nub exposes its neighbours, so wearing one hook out makes
  the next one.
- (4,2) S2 — that a big hop costs more on landing than it saves in the air.

## Row 3 — lower pans. Deeper brine, more of the room underwater.
- (0,3) S7 — that hooking something below the waterline lets you pull under your own
  float line.
- (1,3) S8 — the inverse of (3,0): light, the floor never fails and only the hook does.
- (2,3) S9 — the cascade again, but where the second failure is the useful one.
- (3,3) S5 — that a lip you cannot climb onto floating is trivial to reach heavy.
- (4,3) S6 — that being unable to swim out is a floor, not a wall: you leave sideways.

## Row 4 — the deep pans. Mostly brine. A body at load 4 cannot swim up: be careful.
- (0,4) S7 — that releasing a hook underwater launches you as high as a jump does.
- (1,4) S3 — that two bodies at different loads float at visibly different depths, so
  the pan itself measures you.
- (2,4) S4 — that a row of holes you punched is a row of corners you can hook.
- (3,4) S1 — that dwell time, not weight, is what the crust is counting.
- (4,4) S7 — the deepest pan in the works. Leave the floor of this one bare: no convex
  corner anywhere below the brine line. (PREMISE.md D1 depends on it.)
