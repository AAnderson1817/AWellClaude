# Whole-brief review — craft revision v14

2026-09-20. **Verdict: rework. Overall state: work_in_progress.** The revised stone, rock and face are meaningful improvements over v13. The requested AAA visual finish is still unmet, the authored living-world and discovery scope is incomplete, and human immersion, sensory comfort and wordless understanding remain unverified. Passing preservation and asset checks does not change those dispositions.

This review covers the complete original request: work in the existing repository on a branch; retain a 2D perspective in a 3D treatment with animation-style layered depth; create at least 30 cohesive reference images per environment; rigorously confirm Animal Well design principles; create immersion; and interweave metallurgy, astronomy and faith so that players investigate. It is not restricted to the newest surface or lighting change.

**Authorship disclosure:** I authored the earlier city-response module, original architecture checker and material packer, the separate hunter module/attire, and this increment's editable Blender scanned-stone setup. I did not author the adopted scan, current environment/face geometry or renderer. The geometry and renderer observations below are independent of their makers. My source-material self-review is disclosed, not counted as independent approval. Hunter delivery is not treated as in-game content before integration.

## Findings, in priority order

**1 — Foreground geology remains the largest visual mismatch.** Compared with v13's rounded, mottled rock, the Vault now has clearer cleavage planes and directional breaks. That repairs the earlier inflated appearance. However, the broad triangular faces and straight inset borders around the left wall, floor and ceiling now read as faceted panels placed inside rectangular frames. Their shallow cracks do not yet produce the reference's interlocking mineral masses and locally irregular returns. The inherited stepped landing outline must remain readable; the defect is the construction inside and behind that outline. Improve a representative rock-to-door or rock-to-timber junction at the actual camera before adding more general texture contrast or small rubble.

**2 — Dressed masonry improves, but the hero face still sets a lower finish ceiling.** The city fronts have fewer conspicuous repeated slots, unequal courses, restrained edge damage and a quieter granular surface. The column and facade openings sit more convincingly within that material family than in v13. The change is visible across large surfaces without making the picture busier. Broad front faces and long repeated horizontal joints still expose the modular construction, especially on the lower-right Vault mass and Drowned islands. Local dressed returns, load-bearing transitions and selective wear remain more valuable than indiscriminate extra bevels.

The face now has an actual volumetric nose and mouth, seated orbital bands and mineral grain. It is a much more legible colossal carving than v13's flat wedge nose. Nevertheless the rounded nasal base, cheeks and heavy lower lip/chin still read as softly modeled clay at this camera. The new grain does not resolve those primary forms. The reference's worn cuts and deliberate changes of plane should guide the next sculptural adjustment while preserving the indifferent gaze; anger, cartoon expression or uniformly strong cracks would change the intention. The face remains correctly behind the playable floor and right-hand mass, with its eyes readable and no new apparent platform.

**3 — Incomplete authored responses are a whole-goal blocker.** Inspection of current `main.c`, `LifeStep`, `CityStep` and the prop grid confirms that the hunter module and attire are delivered separately but remain unhooked. The captured camp is therefore still uninhabited. Their module and export checks cannot be counted as a living hunter, synchronized voice/gesture or accepted in-game stone exchange. The aquatic native is also absent. Its indifference, breathing, lamp conversation and stone investigation are central to the premise, rather than optional visual dressing.

The Drowned prop grid remains empty: the specified falling roof pots, throat rope, banners and submerged pack/lamp response are not completed through that system or visible as the authored interactions here. The facade openings, column, cornices, water, windows and fish **are** present and must not be listed as missing. Face-mouth bubbles, the native's breath and the remaining sound pass are still absent. A satisfying ending and invisible deeper discovery structure remain unfinished. Keeping the second verb open follows the premise; inventing a gate or extra mechanic to fill the scene would not repair these gaps.

**4 — Material and light improvements preserve hierarchy, but several interfaces still look provisional.** The new scan gives lit stone a subdued mineral response, with no conspicuous new tile border in these frames. The same-build local-light control differs only modestly: 3.44% of Vault pixels and 1.51% of Drowned pixels change at all; the maximum channel differences are 21/255 and 18/255. That supports a bounded source-light contribution rather than a broad relighting claim. Emissive window panes appear in both controls, so their brightness is not evidence of the new light term.

The square green fixture faces with pale strokes remain flatter and more schematic than their surrounding stone and the reference's housed glass lamps. The concealed mural still leaves a large, pale, scalloped backing slab with an obvious free-standing outline in the Vault. Concealment under nearby light is correct response behavior; the seating of the backing remains a separate craft defect. These transitions weaken the requirement that every element belongs to the same world. The door and astronomical instruments already give metal a clearer identity; preserve that distinction without treating the noninteractive instrument as a new promised control.

**5 — Depth and visual rest survive the changes.** Narrow rear supports leave the distant city open. Foreground landings, the shaft, waterline, floating body and pale shoal remain findable in these images. The water allows the sunken face and floor to remain visible, and quiet intervals around the column prevent an all-over field of detail. The foreground still does not achieve the reference's sculpted depth and rich construction, but increasing fog, bloom, particle density or global brightness would not solve that. Rooted foliage and unequal frond heights help; repeated plant/support rhythms are secondary issues at this stage. The very straight water boundary and simplified reflected body warrant judgment in motion before a still-image preference is turned into a new effect requirement.
