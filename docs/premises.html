Phase 0 · concept gate

Two Verbs, Four Worlds

Six divergent seeds produced twelve premises. Each was put through an emergence stress test before any law-judging: given only a tile grid, a player who can run and jump, and these two verbs with no special-case code, what falls out of the rules? That is the filter Phase 3 will really apply, so it ran first.

These four are the survivors, chosen for maximum difference from each other rather than by score. Each entry ends with what the stress test actually found and what it says is wrong — including the parts that are wrong.

One — The Sounding Works

The Sounding Works

reading vs. rigidity

A dead ore-dressing works of iron gantries and stone hoppers hung across a chasm, where the machinery still runs by itself and everything alive down there is blind and listens through the metal.

A — pure instrument, zero force

A steel tuning fork with a sliding rider weight

First reading
Strike it, press the stem to anything, and watch the vibration travel. Iron rings bright and far, stone drags and dies, packed rust eats the tone instantly. Grit sitting on a plate you are driving crawls into standing-wave patterns and slides along them, so you can walk a pebble across a plate you never touch.
Second reading
The ring maps the insides of things — a wall that answers hollow, two structures that are secretly one piece, a girder whose ripple stops dead three tiles in because there is a crack there. And you are not the only listener: the blind things feel the same metal. The tool that reads the world writes your position into it, and those two facts arrive several rooms apart.

B — pure fastener, zero readout

A joiner's sash cramp

First reading
Set the jaws across two things, turn the screw, and from then on they are one object. Clamp a handhold onto a passing ore-car and ride it. Stop a swinging counterweight. It is one physical cramp, not an ability — clamp it to something that leaves and it leaves with the cramp.
Second reading
Joining two driven bodies stalls them, and the machine does not know it has stalled — strain builds, and the cramp eventually shears off and is flung. A stalled machine is a temporary platform with a countdown you can hear in the pitch. And a cramp can close the two lips of one fracture, which is not joining anything: it is repairing continuity inside a single body.
The coupling

The cramp writes one extra edge into the acoustic bond table the wave stencil already reads. That is the whole interface between the two verbs — one line.

Layer 2 hook

A cast counterweight the size of a house, half-sunk in the first room's floor, a hairline fracture across its face. Tap it early and it gives a dull thud — which the player files under dead scenery, so the game has already answered the question and closed it. It rings only once you have mapped the crack by tapping and know a cramp restores continuity across a fracture.

Indifference

The blind things come to any sustained ring because ringing is what feeding used to sound like. They pass within a tile of you on stone and never register you. The ore-cars run their loop with or without a body on the rail.

What the stress test found

  • Nodal patterns are not additive. Grit parked on a node of plate A stays parked only while A is N tiles long. Cramp plate B onto it and the union is N+M, the node set is entirely different, and grit on a plate you never touched, at a pitch you never changed, gets up and moves.
  • Cracks collect debris by themselves. The gradient force pushes grit toward minima, and an unbonded crack lip is permanently at zero — so every fracture is a global minimum and marks itself with accumulated debris. No art, no flag. Cramping the crack removes the minimum and the pile scatters.
  • A sheared cramp is retrievable by sound. The grit rule tests entities standing on a tile, not what kind. So you can drive a plate from your side of a gap, hold a pitch, and walk your own dropped tool back to you across ground you cannot reach.

Weakest linkThe channel is nearly one-directional: B changes what A reads, but A changes almost nothing about B. And the redundancy cut is held up by a hand-placed carve-out rather than by physics — the grit force is declared to apply to loose entities but not bodies, even though bodies carry velocity in the same struct space. Remove that exception and the fork moves machinery, which collapses L4.

Two — Brine and Ballast

Brine and Ballast

recommended · your own weight as a dial

A drained salt-works of terraced evaporation pans, roofed by collapsing timber scaffold, where blind filter-feeders the size of barges plough slow circuits through the deep brine and everything the sea left behind is still crystallising.

A — scalar magnitudes, gravity axis only

The Kettles — a shoulder yoke of two sealed iron kettles

First reading
Your own weight becomes a dial you turn. Empty, you moon-jump: high, slow, drifting, comically unable to shove anything. Full, you plummet, and a four-tile drop lands with a thud that dimples the crust. A stranger spends a minute at a pan rim just filling and emptying to feel the jump arc stretch.
Second reading
Weight does not scale your movement — it swaps which floors exist. Salt crust holds a light body and shatters under a heavy one, so the light player has a whole walkable plane the heavy player has never seen. The kettles are sealed air vessels: empty you float and cannot descend, full you sink and walk the pan floor. Every room is two rooms and the seam is your own body.

B — positional constraint, adds no energy

The Gaff — a short iron salt-hauling hook

First reading
You bite a corner and become a pendulum. No aiming, no throwing, no return: the hook catches an outside corner within arm's reach or it clacks off. Holding up or down slides your grip along the haft — pull in and you whip faster, pay out and you lengthen. Letting go at the top of an arc and watching where the launch angle comes out is the whole toy.
Second reading
The pivot is a two-body constraint, not a fixed point, and you find out by hooking something that isn't a wall. Hook a drifting raft or a filter-feeder's dorsal plate and you are towed by something that never registered the contact. And corners are bodies too: heavy swings shear salt corners off mid-arc. The world's furniture has a weight class and you are near the bottom of it.
The coupling

Three shared lines, none of them written to be clever: GRAV[load] inside the angular integrator, (1+load) inside the corner-wear counter, and corners[] derived from a tile grid that both verbs delete tiles from.

Layer 2 hook

A cast-iron diving bell, mouth-down on the crust at the lip of the first room's pan, a rusted lifting eye on its crown. In Layer 1 it cannot be pushed, entered, climbed or tipped, and no room is built around it. It becomes usable only after you have failed at the deep pan — because you must be heavy to sink to the floor, cannot climb back out heavy, and there is no rim down there. Hanging off the bell's eye at full load puts a combined weight on the crust beneath it; the crust gives, and the bell goes down with you clinging to it, carrying its trapped air pocket. Inside the pocket there is a rim.

Indifference

The filter-feeders neither pursue nor avoid you. Each ploughs a fixed circuit, mouth open, straining brine; standing in one means being pushed under the crust by a body that never changed course. Riding one is worse, not better: it carries you exactly where it was already going and scrapes you off under a low arch it has been passing for years, at the same speed, with the same rhythm.

What the stress test found

  • Gravity cancels out of the range equation. A full gaff swing throws you exactly as far at every load — the gravity that whips you faster pulls you down sooner. Load selects the shape of the throw at constant distance. Then terminal velocity breaks the tie the wrong way, so the feeble moon-jumping player throws farthest. Every intuition the jump table teaches is inverted by one shared line.
  • Swinging carves its own next anchor. The load stamp is re-applied every frame from the tiles under your feet with no pivot exemption, so at the bottom of each arc you stamp weight onto the crust you skim. It fails in a row of discrete holes at the arc's lowest point — and every hole is a fresh convex corner.
  • Filling destroys its own preconditions. Buoyancy is negative when light and positive when heavy, so a player floating at a rim over deep brine fills one kettle, fills the second, and at the instant load crosses over, the buoyancy holding them in the rim tile inverts and drops them out mid-fill. Over deep brine you are stuck — unless you catch the rim's own corner with the gaff and hang there while you finish. Nothing was written to require both verbs; it falls out of one signed table crossing zero.

Weakest linkThe part the premise is proudest of — the two-body mass ratio — is dead code against static corners, so in most of 25 rooms it degenerates to a plain pendulum and only fires against hand-tuned entities. Cut it and findings 1–7 survive intact. Two defects must be settled before Phase 1: the crust threshold is off by one against its own stated rule, and the gaff currently defeats buoyancy — which dissolves the exact contradiction the diving bell is built on.

Three — Salt and Afterglow

Salt and Afterglow

seeing by subtraction

A disused mineral works cut into terraces, lit only by damp mats of glowing moss that grow wherever water seeps, and grazed by blind animals the size of the room that scrape the walls for salt.

A — read-only, emits nothing

The afterglow slate

First reading
A pane of phosphor-coated slag on a wire handle. Anything bright that passes behind it leaves a glowing smear that fades over about five seconds. Wave it through a luminous drip and it keeps the streak; swing it in a circle and hold a glowing loop in your hand. A five-second-memory etch-a-sketch made of light. It cannot brighten one pixel of the world.
Second reading
It integrates over time, so rhythm becomes geometry — a drip every 1.4s is a dotted line whose spacing you can read off. And when a cell saturates it goes opaque, which makes the slate a hand-held eclipse: cover the one blinding vent and the whole rest of the screen lifts. The tool's real use is subtraction. You make the world dimmer in order to see more.

B — adds matter, subtracts light

The salt gourd

First reading
A falling-sand automaton in your hands. Salt pours, piles at its angle of repose, flows around ledges, pours through a one-tile gap, and a settled pile is solid enough to stand on and walk up. You will fill a crevice for no reason at all and enjoy it.
Second reading
Salt is hygroscopic and this world's light is wet. A grain sitting on a moss tile drains it, and after two seconds the moss goes dark and hard, permanently. The tool that adds matter is the tool that destroys illumination — and you find out by burying a patch you were standing on and watching the room lose a quarter of its brightness while you are still in it.
The coupling

Grains in flight are the only fast bright objects in the world, and the slate integrates them — so a poured stream draws on the slate, and the shape it draws is the trajectory. Burying moss lowers ambient light, which raises exposure, which makes a faint smear finally readable. The destructive verb is the exposure control for the perceptual one.

Layer 2 hook

A shallow stone basin set flush into the first room's floor, its bottom the same dull grey-green as the slate, with a small rusted weight in the middle. You walk across it in the first thirty seconds and it does nothing. It is a slate the size of the floor that has been integrating this room's light since long before you arrived — saturated flat, and read as ordinary stone. Kill every moss tile in that room and its charge starts falling unevenly, because the phosphor under the weight aged differently, and for ten seconds a figure resolves in the floor that nobody drew.

Indifference

The salt-lickers are blind grazers taller than the visible ceiling; you usually see a flank and a tongue entering from one edge. They walk through your position and displace you along the floor — no damage, no reaction, no event at all. The only thing they respond to is salt, so pouring a staircase near one means it turns and eats your staircase while you are standing on it.

What the stress test found

  • Destroying light makes the room brighter. Kill enough moss and the frame's luminance maximum collapses, so auto-exposure applies maximum gain until the dither hash itself becomes the image — and the last surviving bright object is whatever you are pouring. You become the room's light source while holding one tool that emits nothing and another that only destroys light.
  • You can ride your own pile. Pour straight down at your feet; the cell you stand in promotes to solid while your hitbox overlaps it, and depenetration pushes you out along the shallowest axis — upward. A vertical traversal verb nobody designed, produced by the collision resolver being handed a tile that materialised inside the player.
  • A memory budget rendered as a shape. The grain array is a hard cap, so a sustained pour does not get denser when you tilt harder — it gets truncated at a fixed distance from the gourd. The slate integrates that truncation as a line of fixed length. The player can read the size of a fixed-size array off the face of a tool.

Weakest linkFive of the strongest surprises route through one global scalar — the frame's luminance maximum — and that is precisely the variable someone will smooth or clamp the first time the pour-strobe shows up in playtest, because unclamped it is a real photosensitivity hazard. One line of exposure smoothing turns this pair from rich to thin in an afternoon, and whoever writes it will think they are fixing a rendering bug. Separately, salt piles live in the room arena while moss death is a persistent tile write, so salt builds nothing that survives a screen transition — which quietly removes it from the traversal vocabulary across all 25 rooms.

Four — The Proving Floor

The Proving Floor

volume and force vs. adhesion and memory

A bakehouse the size of a cathedral that nobody has staffed in a very long time — still warm, still running, its lower halls silted knee-deep in old flour.

A — volume, force, time

A lump of live leaven

First reading
Set it down anywhere and it swells. Soft-bodied: you sink into it, it takes your shape, it squeezes through a gap and comes out elongated, it lifts anything light resting on top, it plugs a hole airtight. People laugh the first time they sit on one and slowly rise. It grows faster where the floor is warm and stops dead where it is cold, which anyone works out in seconds.
Second reading
It has a clock. Left too long in heat it over-proofs and collapses into slack, and slack dough is not a solid — it is a fluid that seeks the lowest point, pours through gratings the intact lump could never fit, and re-thickens once it reaches somewhere cold. The same object is a jack, a plug, a platform, a timer, and, if you deliberately cook it too long, a liquid you can decant somewhere you cannot reach.

B — adhesion, visibility, memory

A tin flour dredger

First reading
Sift and a slow cloud comes off, drifts on whatever air the room has, and settles into drifts on ledges. It has no weight and no collision — it cannot hold anything up. It is pure pleasure: shake it and the room's invisible air becomes legible, curling around a doorway, spiralling above a warm tile. Kick a drift and it puffs.
Second reading
Flour is a recording medium, and what it records is not yours. Dust a hall, leave, come back: there are tracks across it that are not your size and not your gait, going somewhere. And the real inversion — dough will not anchor to a floured surface, so the useful tool is often the removal of flour, and the game never says so.
The coupling

Dough anchors only where there is no dust, and expansion pushes off its anchor set. Flour under a lump and symmetric growth becomes net horizontal thrust — and because a sliding blob wipes dust behind it, it re-anchors the instant it runs off the end. The length of the floured strip is exactly the distance the lump travels, which is a rule you can measure with a shaker and then aim.

Layer 2 hook

A long shallow stone trough at the base of the first room's wall, filled flush with a grey, rock-hard set mass — a sponge left to die decades ago. You walk over it for the entire game. It is not dead, only dry, cold, and by the end buried under the flour you have been dusting into that room since minute one. It wakes only on bare contact with live leaven along its length, which requires the one action that both wipes it clean and delivers dough: sliding a floured lump down it.

Indifference

Nothing is hunting. The machinery keeps handling material and you are material: millstones turn, a conveyor of trays goes into a dark aperture and comes back empty, and all of it processes a body exactly as it processes a lump. The mill-moths are dog-sized, want spilled flour, and walk over you to get to it. Their feet are cold.

What the stress test found

  • Footprints steer dough. Walking decrements dust, and dough anchors only where dust is zero — so a lump grown in a dusted room anchors only on tiles you happened to walk on, and its thrust is the sum of normals under your own path. Two identical rooms, two walking routes, two launch directions. The number of times you have walked somewhere becomes a physical quantity.
  • Size is instability. A blob growing across a dusted floor loses anchors as it gets bigger, because every new frontier cell in a floured tile is dead weight. Past some radius the anchored fraction crosses zero and the whole thing launches with no input at all. In a dusty room dough has a maximum stable diameter, which you discover by having a lump leave without permission.
  • Dough climbs. Thrust is the sum of inward normals, and slope tiles have diagonal normals — so a lump grown into an inside corner of two slopes has a residual with a vertical component, and de-anchoring it sends it straight up indefinitely. No line of code says dough climbs. Slopes were in the tile set before either verb existed.

Weakest linkVerb A's vent-and-pressure second reading cannot happen: wind is authored per room and never written at runtime, so rerouting pressure needs either a solver (outside the envelope) or hand-coded two-port vents — a special case, which is exactly what this filter exists to catch. Cut it. Behind that, an unanchored blob is exempt from gravity as specified, so a mis-aimed lump drifts off-screen forever and needs a fall rule before any sandbox session is meaningful.

Recommendation

Brine and Ballast

and what would have to be true in Phase 3

Not because it scored highest — Salt and Afterglow found more surprises and is the more beautiful idea. Because of where its surprises come from and what it costs to find out.

  1. The emergence sits in shared variables, not in authored content. The stress test's own verdict is that the pitched feature — the two-body mass ratio — is the weak part, and that the real richness comes from three duller shared lines. That is exactly the right shape for L2. Compare Salt and Afterglow, whose five best surprises all hang off one global scalar that a renderer fix would quietly delete.
  2. It is the only one I am confident we can play with early. No field simulation: lookup tables indexed by load, a corner array built once per room, one pendulum integrator. The others risk spending Phases 2 and 3 debugging a simulation instead of playing with toys — and Phase 3's gate is five surprises found by playing, not by reasoning.
  3. Both verbs pass gate (a) hard. Filling and emptying at a rim to feel the jump arc stretch; releasing a swing at different angles to see where you go. Both are fun in thirty seconds with no goal attached.
  4. L12 is arithmetic, not fiction. The jump table teaches heavy = powerful for hours. Then gravity cancels out of the swing's range equation and the light body throws farthest. Guiding the player into thinking they fully understand something, then revealing that they don't — as a consequence of one shared constant.
  5. L4 splits cleanly. The kettles change scalar magnitudes along the gravity axis and cannot attach you to anything. The gaff adds no energy and alters no property of your body. Where they look like they overlap — both get you up — is where they are most cleanly divided: weight raises your apex and cannot cross a gap, the gaff crosses gaps and can never lift you above the corner you caught.

The honest risk. The Layer 2 hook depends on a contradiction the gaff may dissolve. The diving bell's gate is “heavy enough to sink, too heavy to climb out, no rim down there” — but the pivot integrator reads gravity and never buoyancy, so hooking a submerged corner lets you ratchet down and back up and the contradiction evaporates anywhere a submerged corner exists. That has to be settled deliberately before Phase 1, not discovered in Phase 5: either buoyancy enters the submerged pivot, which kills a good emergent behaviour and saves the bell, or the bell's gate gets rebuilt on something the gaff cannot reach. Second risk, smaller: two verbs that both act on your own body means the world changes less than in the other three. Crust destruction and corner wear are the only persistent edits, and the room arena wipes crust on transition — so it recrystallises when you return. That is a defensible choice, but it needs to be a choice.

How this was produced, and where to distrust it

Six seeds, each given a different generative constraint, produced two premises apiece. Each of the twelve went through an independent emergence stress test before any law-judging, then two adversarial judges working from opposite directions.

The filter did not discriminate. All twelve came back “rich”, and the judges have been averaging 7–8 with no blocking violations. A 12/12 pass rate means the scores are not doing the work, so the selection here is mine, made by reading all twelve in full — not the panel's ranking. The stress tests earned their keep anyway: their surprise lists and weakest-link critiques are specific, mechanical, and in several places found defects the premises did not know they had.

Two runners-up worth pulling up if none of these land: Dry Measure (a surveyor's spring tape whose stiffness is decided by what its tip touches, paired with grit that is a friction dial — the retract pulls whichever end has less friction, and you do not get to choose, the floor does) and The Long Thaw (a felted blanket that is a stiffness state machine, paired with a block of lake ice — you use the coldest thing in the world to build a machine that delivers heat). The Long Thaw is the best-written of the twelve and the biggest build risk.
