### Sounding Works rich 8
  * amp[] is indexed in room-grid space, but body tiles translate through that grid. When a driven body moves, the tiles it vacates keep their cur/prev values and are no longer bonded to anything, so each becomes an isolated oscillator decaying only by the damping term. A ringing ore-car therefore lays a fading trail of vibration in the empty floor behind it, and when its loop brings it back through its own wake the stencil sums a live driver against stale residue and produces beat patterns that have no source. Loose grit sitting in that trail gets a gradient kick from a wave whose emitter left the room.
  * A sheared cramp lies on the floor as a loose entity. The grit rule tests entities standing on a tile, not what kind of entity they are, so `vel += -grad(|amp|)*s` applies to the cramp exactly as it applies to a pebble. You can drive a plate from your side of a gap, hold a pitch, and walk your own dropped tool back to you across ground you cannot reach. This is the only channel by which verb A affects verb B at all, and nobody wrote it.
  * Closing the cramp appends a bond edge to a stencil that is already running. The instant the edge exists, `next = 2*cur - prev + k*(sum of bonded neighbours - 4*cur)` gains a neighbour term that may differ by the full amplitude, injecting a step function. Cramping while the near body sits at an antinode produces a transient far louder than anything the fork can drive; cramping at a node is silent. Since the rider sets the period, closing the screw becomes a timed percussive act with a phase window — a way to make the loudest event in the works with a tool that has no sound of its own.
  * Nodal positions depend on the bonded length of the resonator, and cramping changes that length. Grit you carefully parked on a node of plate A stays parked only while A is N tiles long. Cramp plate B onto it and the union is N+M tiles, the node set is entirely different, and the grit on the plate you never touched, at a pitch you never changed, gets up and moves. The nodal pattern of a joined body is not the union of its parts' patterns, which is not obvious until you watch it happen.
  * The gradient force pushes toward minima of |amp|. An unbonded crack edge means the far lip is permanently at zero, so every crack in the works is a global minimum of the field for the body it sits in. Any loose grit on a body you drive migrates into its fractures and stays there. Fractures therefore mark themselves with accumulated debris as a consequence of physics, with no art or flag doing it — and cramping the crack removes the minimum, so the pile that took a minute to gather scatters in a second.
  * `next -= next>>6` on a signed i16 does nothing when |next| < 64, and arithmetic right shift on negatives rounds toward -inf, so small negative values bleed off by one per frame while small positive values do not decay at all. Struck material settles to a permanent low-level positive ring instead of silence, and the field acquires a slow positive bias everywhere it has ever been driven. Consequence in play: the works never goes fully quiet, residual gradients keep grit creeping in rooms you left, and the blind things drift toward places that are ringing because of nothing.
  * Union-find over `u8 parent[32]` has no notion of infinite mass. Cramping a driven body to static terrain merges a set containing exactly one driver, so the 'conflicting drivers' zeroing never fires and nothing stops the driver from continuing to integrate velocity for the whole merged set. Unless terrain is special-cased out, an ore-car cramped to a hopper frame tows the hopper — you move architecture with a screw. The sandbox forces this resolution within a minute of play, and either answer (car stops dead, or the room walks) is a mechanic nobody chose.
  * Shear direction is not authored — the cramp is flung along whatever the accumulated driver force was doing. That makes deliberate over-straining an aiming problem: pick which pair of bodies to stall and you pick which way your tool gets thrown. In a game with no throw verb and no way to cross certain gaps, the machine's own power becomes the delivery mechanism for the only object you own, and the fork (via the grit rule) becomes the retrieval mechanism. A transport loop exists that neither verb contains.
  WEAKEST: The coupling is a single edge in a bond table indexed in world-tile space, while the cramp's whole identity is joining things that MOVE — the one point of contact between the two verbs is built on the one coordinate system that fails the instant either half translates. Fix is body-relative edge storage, and it must be fixed before any sandbox session is meaningful. Structurally worse: the channel is nearly one-directional. B changes what A reads; A changes nothing about B except by the accident of a dropped cramp being loose matter. And the redundancy cut is held up by a hand-placed carve-out, not by physics — the grit force is declared to apply to 'loose entities' but not to bodies, even though bodies carry vx,vy in the same struct space. Remove that exception and the fork moves machinery, which collapses L4. The pair is rich, but its richness currently rests on one exception clause and one broken index.

### Brine and Ballast rich 7
  * A full gaff swing throws you exactly as far regardless of load, because GRAV[load] cancels out of the range equation. Release speed at the bottom is v = sqrt(2*GRAV[load]*r*(1-cos(theta0))) and ballistic flight time is sqrt(2h/GRAV[load]); the gravity that whips you faster is the same gravity that pulls you down sooner, so range = 2*sqrt(r*h*(1-cos(theta0))) with no load term at all. Load selects the SHAPE of the throw — load 4 is fast and flat and arrives early, load 0 is slow and high and arrives late — at constant distance. Then TERM_V[load] breaks the tie the wrong way: the low terminal velocity at load 0 stretches the descent, so the feeble moon-jumping player actually throws FARTHEST off the hook. Every intuition the jump table teaches (heavy = powerful) is inverted by the one line that shares GRAV between the two systems.
  * Load is a second parametric pump channel that nobody built. The integrator has no mass term — omega is untouched when load changes, only the field it accelerates in changes — so filling at the top of an arc and emptying at the bottom does net positive work every half cycle, exactly like the intended pull-in/pay-out radius pump but through an unrelated variable. The single-shot version is the strong one: hang, swing empty for the launch (or full, then dump), and change load in the instant before release. Release velocity was computed under the OLD gravity, the ballistic arc runs under the NEW one, so a full-load whip released into a load-0 fall is a launch speed the light body could never generate carried by a fall the heavy body could never sustain — the longest flight available in the game, out of two systems that separately cannot do it. It self-limits honestly: 20 frames per kettle against a ~60-frame swing period, and only where the arc keeps you inside a TF_RIM tile, so it stays a discovered trick rather than a broken exploit.
  * The corner-wear counter, which exists for flavour ('the world remembers a bad idea for forty seconds'), silently becomes the balance patch for the pumping exploit. worn += (1+load) every 8 pivot frames means load 0 has ~256 pivot frames of hook life and load 4 has ~51. Pumping requires being heavy on the descent, which is precisely the state that eats the corner fastest — the energy you gain is paid for in anchor lifetime by the same variable, so the exploit throttles itself with no balancing code. And the failure mode is not a clean stop: at 32 the tile is swapped out and you are dropped MID-ARC, launched at whatever theta the counter happened to expire on. The most powerful thing you can do with the gaff is also the thing that guarantees you will not choose your own release angle.
  * Verb A is a level editor for verb B, in both directions, because corners[] is derived from tiles[][] and both verbs delete tiles. Standing heavy on crust deletes crust tiles, which manufactures new convex corners on the surviving neighbours — so the load-4 player breaks the ground precisely to create the hooks the load-0 player needs, and a room's anchor set is a function of where you have recently stood. Worse (better): when a worn nub is swapped out, its neighbours become convex, so demolishing one hook exposes two more one tile deeper. A heavy player can chew a staircase of pivots sideways into a salt shelf, swinging through solid mass on hooks that are each created by destroying the previous one. If the corners array is NOT rebuilt after a tile swap, the alternate outcome is just as unplanned: stale entries leave you hanging from a corner in open air.
  * Swinging carves a dotted line through crust that traces your own pendulum geometry. loadMap is cleared and re-stamped every frame from 'the one or two tiles under its feet' with no pivot-state exemption, so at the bottom of each arc — the only part of the swing where your feet graze the floor — you stamp 1+load onto whatever crust you skim. Stress accumulates only during those few frames per pass, so the crust fails in a row of discrete holes at exactly the arc's lowest point, spaced by nothing you drew, and then chains into the previous finding: each hole is a fresh corner. A player pumping a swing over shallow crust is auto-generating the next anchor beneath themselves without ever touching a design decision.
  * The gaff defeats buoyancy, which why_not_redundant explicitly claims it cannot do. The pivot integrator reads GRAV[load] and never SINK[load], and pivot state replaces free movement, so hooking a submerged corner at load 0 swaps you out of the buoyancy solver into an air-gravity pendulum: you hang, and you can pull yourself DOWNWARD against a float that the empty kettles are supposed to make inescapable. Release restores SINK[0] and you shoot back up, so a submerged wall becomes a ratchet — hook, swing down, hook the next corner below before releasing, repeat. This is a genuine consequence of one system overriding the other's integration path, and it is also a leak: the deep pan's 'you cannot come back up at load 4' contradiction, which the entire Layer 2 bell is built on, evaporates anywhere a submerged corner exists.
  * The fill action destroys its own preconditions at exactly the sign flip, and the other verb is the only fix. Filling requires holding position on a TF_RIM tile; SINK[load] is negative at 0-1 and positive at 2+. So a player floating at a rim over deep brine fills kettle one, fills kettle two, and at the instant load crosses to 2 the buoyancy that was holding them in the rim tile inverts and drops them out of it mid-fill. Over deep brine you are pinned at load 2 forever — unless you catch the rim's own convex corner with the gaff and hang there while you finish filling. Nothing was written to require both verbs to reach load 3; it falls out of one signed lookup table crossing zero inside the window where a different verb needs you to stay put.
  WEAKEST: The two-body mu constraint — the part the premise is proudest of. For static tile corners anchorMass = 1e9 makes mu identically zero, so in an empty sandbox and in most of 25 rooms the entire two-way path is dead code that degenerates to a plain pendulum; it only fires against authored entities with hand-tuned mass, and its advertised outcomes ('at load 2 the raft tips and pours') are thresholded special cases. Cut it and you lose nothing from findings 1-7; keep it and you are budgeting a physics system to serve content. The genuine emergence in this pair comes from three much duller shared lines: GRAV[load] inside the angular integrator, (1+load) inside the wear counter, and corners[] being derived from a tile grid that both verbs delete tiles from. Protect those three and the pair is rich. TWO DEFECTS THAT MUST BE FIXED BEFORE PHASE 1: (1) loadMap off-by-one — the stamp is 1+load and the crust threshold is loadMap >= 2, so load 1 shatters crust, contradicting verbA_second_reading's 'holds a body of load 0-1'; either stamp load or raise the threshold to 3, but note that raising it also changes what 'resting props stamp their own constant' means. (2) The gaff defeats buoyancy (finding 6) because the pivot integrator reads GRAV[load] and never SINK[load] — this contradicts why_not_redundant's 'cannot sink you in brine' and dissolves the deep-pan contradiction the Layer 2 bell depends on. Decide deliberately: either add SINK to the submerged pivot (kills a good emergent, saves the bell) or keep it and rebuild the bell's gate on something the gaff cannot reach. Do not leave it undecided.

### Salt and Afterglow rich 12
  * The mask is an axis-aligned bounding box over ALL opaque cells, so masking behaviour is not local. Hold the slate near a bright vent; one corner cell hits 255; that cell joins the opaque set; the bounding rect snaps outward to include it; the rect now excludes MORE pixels from the frame's luminance maximum; the global exposure lifts; every remaining cell's sampled src goes up; those cells now charge faster than the -1/frame decay can drain them; they saturate; the rect grows again. The slate whites itself out in a two-second cascade from a single stray bright pixel. The failure is discontinuous and irreversible until you walk the slate into darkness and wait out the full 4.25s decay.
  * Grains are the only fast bright objects in the room and the exposure normalises to the brightest thing CURRENTLY VISIBLE, recomputed per frame. A pour is a discontinuous stream of bright objects entering and leaving frame at the pour rate. So the whole room's exposure oscillates at the grain-arrival frequency: the entire 320x180 image strobes in time with the pour. Tilt the gourd harder and the strobe frequency rises. Nobody wrote a strobe; it is the auto-exposure sampling a stochastic max. This is also very likely a photosensitivity defect that will need a clamp, and the clamp is the thing that kills half this list.
  * Salt-kill every moss tile in the sandbox and the frame's luminance maximum collapses toward zero. An exposure that normalises to the brightest visible thing then applies maximum gain: the room gets BRIGHTER the more light you destroy, until the dither hash itself becomes the image. Worse, the last surviving bright object is whatever you are pouring — so the room's global exposure is now driven by your own grain stream. Pour and the screen dims (grains become the max); stop and it blows out. The player has become the room's light source while holding a tool that explicitly emits nothing and a tool that only destroys light.
  * grains[64] is a hard cap. At ~2-3 px/frame a full-height fall occupies a grain for 60-90 frames, so the array saturates about a second into any sustained pour. The stream therefore does not get denser when you tilt harder - it gets TRUNCATED at a fixed distance from the gourd, because new grains cannot spawn until old ones deposit. The slate integrates that truncation as a line of fixed length regardless of pour rate. The fixed-size preallocated array becomes visible geometry the player can read off the slate's face. A memory budget rendered as a shape.
  * Salt promotes a cell to SOLID at fill>=6, and the promotion overwrites whatever collision that tile had. Pour onto a slope and once six subcells settle, the slope becomes a full solid block: the smooth run-up you were using to clear a gap is now a 1-tile vertical ledge that kills your speed. Salt does not only add traversal, it deletes it. You can permanently (for the room's lifetime) break a jump you had already been making, using the tool whose whole first reading is 'build a ramp'.
  * Pour straight down at your own feet. The cell you are standing in reaches fill>=6 and promotes to SOLID while the player's AABB overlaps it. Standard depenetration resolves by pushing the player out along the shallowest axis - upward. Repeat and you ride your own pile up a tile at a time. A vertical traversal verb nobody designed, produced entirely by the collision resolver being handed a tile that materialised inside the player. On a slope the push-out resolves sideways instead and you get shoved along the incline, which reads exactly like the grazer displacement in the indifference clause, from a completely unrelated cause.
  * The slate point-samples world pixels per frame with no motion blur. Standing still, a 1.4s drip is a dot. Walking, it is a dashed line whose gap length equals your velocity times 1.4s. Running, the dashes spread further. But the interesting case is two periodic emitters in frame at once: their sample patterns beat against each other and against your own footspeed, producing moire fringes on the slate face that shift as you accelerate. Identical-looking drips at different phases resolve as visibly different fringe patterns. The premise claims 'rhythm becomes geometry'; the actual sampler makes it 'rhythm times your velocity becomes geometry', which is a stronger and stranger statement the designer did not write down.
  * The player carries rim light, and the slate samples world pixels at its own held position a few px in front of the body. The 24-cell span therefore overlaps the player's own lit silhouette edge every frame you are lit at all. The slate's leading cells charge from YOU, saturate from just being carried, and then the bounding-box mask opaques the entire face. The tool degrades by being held. Correct use requires either keeping the slate in shadow or holding it out at arm's length, which is a handling discipline that emerges from the sampler's position rather than from any rule about the slate.
  * Grains are the brightest fast thing in the world, so pouring anywhere near the slate is by far the fastest way to drive cells to 255. But saturation is exactly what converts the slate from a drawing surface into an eclipse. So the drawing function and the eclipse function are in direct competition through a single shared variable, and verb B is the fastest way to destroy verb A's second reading. Using the pen ruins the paper. The player learns to pour AWAY from the slate to keep it usable, which is the opposite of the pitched 'B is the pen, A is the paper' relationship.
  * fill[22][40] lives in the room arena and is wiped on transition; tiles[y][x] = TILE_MOSS_DEAD is a persistent tile write. Walk off screen and back: your salt staircase is gone, the moss you buried is still dead. Every structure salt builds is temporary; every consequence salt causes is permanent. The verb gives nothing lasting and takes everything permanently, and it does so with no code expressing that intent - it falls straight out of which memory scope each array was assigned to. It also means salt cannot gate anything across a room boundary, which quietly deletes half the traversal design space.
  * The pitched coupling: burying moss lowers the room's ambient light, which lowers the frame max, which raises exposure, which makes a previously-drowned faint smear readable on the slate. It does work and needs no special-case code. The unstated sharpening is that the charge rule is g += src>>2 against a flat -1/frame decay, so any source with src<=4 records NOTHING, ever. Exposure lift does not fade sources in gradually - it snaps them across a hard threshold. The slate goes from permanently blank to actively drawing with no intermediate state, which players will read as 'the slate turned on' rather than 'the room got brighter'.
  * The drying rule is `if (fill[y][x] && EMISSIVE) if(--wet[y][x]==0) kill`. It never consults water. So salt sinking to the bottom of a pool kills submerged moss in the same two seconds, and there is no re-wetting rule anywhere in the implementation - the premise's 'irreversible unless water reaches the tile again' is an escape clause with zero code behind it. Drying is therefore unconditional and monotonic across the whole 25-room world. A player who empties the gourd methodically can strip-mine the game's entire lighting into a state with no recovery path, and the world will keep auto-exposing upward to compensate until the dither is the only remaining image.
  WEAKEST: Verb A is strictly read-only, so every one of its interactions with verb B has to route through ONE global scalar: the frame's luminance maximum. That single variable carries surprises 1, 2, 3, 9 and 11 - the five strongest on the list. It is also the variable a renderer programmer will instinctively smooth, clamp, or hysteresis-filter the first time the pour-strobe (surprise 2) shows up in a playtest, because unclamped it is a genuine photosensitivity hazard. One line of exposure smoothing turns this pair from rich to thin in an afternoon, and the person who writes that line will think they are fixing a rendering bug, not deleting five of the twelve surprises. The pair's entire richness is a single point of failure sitting in a file nobody will think of as design code. Two secondary weaknesses: (a) the implementation never specifies whether the slate samples pre- or post-exposure luminance, and the pitch needs BOTH - post-exposure for the second-order coupling it advertises, pre-exposure for its claim that the slate honestly reads what is bright; (b) putting fill[][] in the room arena while moss death writes persistently to tiles[][] means salt builds nothing that survives a screen transition and destroys everything permanently, which is emergent and interesting but also silently removes salt from the traversal-gating vocabulary across all 25 rooms. Separately, three of the five affordance_examples and the headline image of key_interaction (grains outlining your shoulders) require collision between grains and objects that exist in neither grid - they are features being marketed as emergence, and the pair does not need them, which is worth saying out loud before someone budgets for them.

### Proving Floor rich 8
  * Feet on dust>0 decrement the tile, and dough anchors only where dust==0. So walking is a de-flouring brush: pacing back and forth over a floured field four times zeroes those tiles and nothing else. A lump grown in the middle of a dusted room therefore anchors ONLY on the tiles the player happened to walk on, and its thrust vector is the sum of normals under the player's own path. Two identical rooms, two different walking routes, two different launch directions. Nobody coded 'footprints steer dough'; it falls out of dust-decrement-on-step meeting anchor-requires-dust-zero. The number of times you have walked somewhere becomes a physical quantity.
  * A blob expands by claiming free 4-neighbours, and those neighbours are in tiles that may carry settled dust. Anchoring is evaluated per cell against its tile. So a blob growing across a dusted floor loses anchors as it gets BIGGER — every new frontier cell in a floured tile is dead weight, while the original anchored cells stay a fixed count. Past some radius the anchored fraction crosses zero and the whole thing launches with no player input at all. Size is instability. In a dusty room dough has a maximum stable diameter that the player will discover by having a lump leave without permission.
  * Motes take a repulsion term from anything moving faster than 1 px/frame, and an expanding blob is exactly that. So a blob growing on a floured floor shoves the airborne flour outward, and those motes then settle — into a ring of raised dust on the tiles immediately ahead of the expansion frontier. The blob bulldozes a berm of its own anchor-poison into the ground it is about to grow onto. Growth on flour is self-terminating in a way neither rule intends: it clears where it is and poisons where it is going.
  * Thrust is the sum of inward normals of anchored cells, and slope tiles have diagonal normals. A lump grown into the underside of a ceiling wedge, or into an inside corner made of two slopes, has a residual with a vertical component. De-anchor it with flour and the blob translates at 8 px/s diagonally or straight UP, indefinitely, because unanchored translation is along the residual and nothing in the rules makes a free blob fall. Dough climbs. No line of code says dough climbs; slopes were in the tile set before either verb existed.
  * With zero anchors the normal sum is zero, so the residual has to be latched from the last frame that still had anchors. That means the launch direction is decided by WHICH ANCHOR DIES LAST — i.e. by the order the player sifts, not by the final flour layout. Dust the floor left-to-right and the lump goes one way; dust the identical floor right-to-left and it goes the other. Same end state, opposite result. A player will set up the same rig twice, get two answers, and have to work out that the shaker has a stroke order.
  * heat[] is sampled from tile flags, and a lump left on a hot tile crusts into permanent level geometry — a new solid tile. That new tile sits on the hot one and the heat sample now reads the crust. The player can permanently brick the room's only oven with the same action that gives them a new ledge, and the two outcomes are the same button held for the same duration. Irreversible, unannounced, and produced by two rules written for unrelated reasons.
  * A SLIDING blob wipes dust to 0 on every tile it crosses. Slack dough is not sliding — it is a falling-sand fluid with its own rule — so it cleans nothing. Pour slack across a floured floor into a cold pocket and it re-thickens into a solid lump with zero anchors from birth and no residual, so it neither grips nor moves: an inert free lump. Slide the same mass over the same floor and it grips the instant it stops. Identical material, identical destination, opposite result depending purely on which state it travelled in. The player will pour first, get a dead lump, and have no idea why.
  * Motes are repelled by any entity moving faster than 1 px/frame — including the player. Sift, then run through your own cloud before it settles and you push the flour where you ran, so run speed and route become the aiming mechanism for dust placement, and therefore for where the next blob will and will not grip. Conversely, standing still while sifting settles dust evenly around you and prints your own silhouette as an absence. Holding still and running are two different tools and neither was written as one.
  WEAKEST: Verb A's vent/pressure second reading. \"A lump grown into a vent traps what the vent is pushing, and the pressure has to go somewhere else\" cannot happen: wind is `static i8 wind[20*11*2]`, authored per room and never written to at runtime. Rerouting pressure requires either a solver (outside the envelope) or hand-coded two-port vent objects — a special case, which is exactly what this filter exists to catch. Cut it, or demote vents to explicit paired source/sink entities where plugging one flips a flag on its partner and admit that it is authored. Two smaller fragilities behind it: (1) the anchored-count-0 residual is undefined by the spec and only works via a latch, and an unanchored blob is also exempt from gravity, so a mis-aimed lump drifts off-screen forever — this needs a decay or a fall rule before Phase 3 or the sandbox will be full of escaped dough; (2) `dust` is per-tile (1 byte) but `tread` is 8 sub-columns, so footprints read at a resolution four times finer than they act at — the most legible thing on screen is physically the coarsest, which will misteach the anchor rule unless dust is moved to the 4px subgrid the dough already uses.
