# The archive: the building's language

The six screens are one building. This file is its architecture: what its makers were
asked to do, what they put first, and the few parts everything is built from. The lore is
`claude/LORE.md`; where things are is `claude/ANTECHAMBER.md`. If a screen has something in
it that is not made of these parts, it does not belong to this building.

## The brief its makers were given

Keep every living thing alive, for as long as it takes, through a cold that does not end,
with no one left to tend it.

## What they put first

1. **Outlast us.** Cut into the living rock and grow the rest. Nothing assembled that can
   be carved; few moving parts; mass. Every surface is either cut from the rock or grown
   out of it, and the seam between the two is where the story shows.
2. **A steady heart.** One warm core at the bottom (the hearth, beyond the fireguard), the
   water as its store. The building is a gradient round it: warm and alive near the heart,
   cold and asleep at the edges.
3. **Feed every cell.** Veins of their glass in the stone, and roots grown along them,
   carry warmth and light from the heart to everything. They are visible everywhere and
   they all run one way. Following them is the way through.
4. **One unit, every scale.** The *cell*: a round opening closed by an iris. Eight px, it is
   a seed drawer. Sixty, a specimen bay. A hundred and seventy, the door. Three hundred,
   the colossus's niche. When you see that the drawer by your foot is the door's shape,
   the building's size arrives.
5. **A catalogue without words.** Order is shape, size and light. A cell is the size of what
   it holds. Its light says its state: **bright, living; dim, asleep; dark, only the
   pattern is left.** The walls are the index.

## How the life is kept: three tiers

- **Living**: gardens and the basin under grown light. The only warm, green, moving part.
- **Sleeping**: cold stacks of seeds, spores, eggs in glass drawers, in the walls everywhere.
- **Pattern**: when a thing cannot be kept, the glass keeps its shape and its warmth,
  cold. This is what their green light is.

**The truth under it (quiet, never shown as an event).** The heart has been cooling for
ages; the archive cannot make warmth. It has been moving its collection down the tiers --
living to sleeping to pattern -- and the stacks go dark from the edges in. Then warm
things come down the chimney. The archive does to them what it does to everything it
keeps: it catalogues them. The prints on the door are the first entry in your record. The
hunter at the camp is being kept living. The sitters on the sill are being moved to
sleep. The bones are pattern. Nothing is done to anyone on screen; the stages are
different people, and the player joins them up (LORE.md section 6 still holds).

## The parts

- **Rib.** A pier of stone drums stacked like vertebrae, a root grown up it in a spiral, a
  vein of glass in a channel on its face, a node of glass at each joint. Ribs rise from
  the floor and lean in at the top, into arches that close out of sight overhead. They
  divide the building into bays; each screen is framed by them.
- **Cell.** A round opening, a moulded ring, an iris of blades, a lens at the centre whose
  light is the cell's state. The same drawing at every size: at seed size the iris is a
  dot, at door size it is nine blades.
- **Stacks.** The wall's fabric: cells in courses, each course on a shelf, in their
  thousands, receding. Living few, sleeping many, dark more toward the edges.
- **Vein.** A line of their light cut into the stone, running toward the heart. Pulses move
  along it now and then, always the same way: the archive reading.
- **Root.** What grows along the veins: warm browns, thick, gripping, mossed where light
  reaches. The living part of the structure, never the decoration of it.
- **Rock.** Raw strata where the archive is cut and has not yet grown: the edges, and
  where the mountain has pushed back in.

## Scale, shown

- The cell at every size (above).
- **Three makers, three sizes**: the hunters' planks and ropes (your size), the tall ones'
  stair and door (theirs), the archive's cells and ribs (neither's).
- **Never show an end**: every screen edge cuts through something that continues -- ribs
  past the top, stacks through the floor, veins into the rock.
- **Light as distance**: small lights far off in the stacks, pools of light separated by
  long dark.
- The great window looks out on more archive, not a city: stacks of lit cells to the
  horizon.

## The six bays

| screen | bay | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| A | intake | the door, the largest cell, framed by the first ribs; the forecourt walls are seed drawers; the prints are you being entered |
| B | the keeper | the colossus, seated in a cell like every other, hand out to receive; the mural is the index |
| C | the stacks window | the view out is the archive itself |
| D | seed vault | cold drawers floor to roof; the hunters camp here, out of the draft |
| E | the living garden | the basin and its terraces under grown light |
| F | the heart | the fireguard is the grate over the warm core; the giant stair is terraced beds |

Built first: **A**, as the proof of the language. The others follow once it is seen.
